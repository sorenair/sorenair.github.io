# Portfolio design references

The portfolio should feel like a retro-futurist digital artifact. Build that identity through the Ø, precise typography, aligned panels, grayscale surfaces, and one accent drawn from the active content. Give motion a job. A heading can introduce Soren, an ASCII mask can reveal the reel, and a change in focus can make the selected project easier to follow. Keep the page structure and controls familiar across Home, Work, About, and Media. [Shared style guide](redesign/SHELL.md)

## Component studies

| Reference | What to draw from it and how to apply it |
| --- | --- |
| **ASCII Effect** | **Lead hero study.** The renderer maps image luminance to glyphs and offers posterization, dithering, and pointer ripples. Use a frame from Soren’s reel to make a recognizable monochrome image, with a low-resolution glyph field and a subtle response near the pointer. Generate a still version from the same frame for reduced motion and initial load. [Componentry](https://componentry.dev/docs/components/ascii-effect) |
| **Kinetic Text Reveal** | **Lead typography study.** Borrow the short directional reveal and restrained stagger. Animate the words or lines of the Home title once with a clipped wrapper and a small CSS translation. Render the heading as real text from the first paint and keep **View work** available throughout. Test the reveal independently of the ASCII motion before combining them. [Componentry](https://componentry.dev/docs/components/kinetic-text-reveal) |
| **Pixel Image Trail** | **Hero interaction study.** The demo paints fragments of a still image that fade out. Adapt that behavior to clear small cells of the ASCII mask and expose the reel poster underneath. Give touch and keyboard users a visible **Play reel** button that clears the full mask and starts playback. [Componentry](https://componentry.dev/docs/components/pixel-image-trail) |
| **Liquid Glass Carousel** | **Featured projects study.** Borrow the weighted drag, snap to center, and sense of depth for a small selection of Work entries. Start with a horizontal row of linked thumbnails, CSS scroll snap, and Previous and Next controls. If refraction earns its place, test it inside this row and retain the links and controls in HTML. Use the settled active item to update the accent. [Componentry](https://componentry.dev/docs/components/liquid-glass-carousel) · [Original carousel engine](https://github.com/Yousuf-developer/liquid-glass-carousel) |
| **Fisheye Infinite Grid** | **Media exploration study.** The WebGL wall repeats tiles and lets visitors pan in two axes. Try the curvature and loose browsing on a dedicated Media page with a regular gallery available from the same content. Keep the canvas contained so the surrounding page scrolls normally. Give photos, art, and video real HTML links and labels. [Componentry](https://componentry.dev/docs/components/fisheye-infinite-grid) |
| **Signature** | **About detail.** The demo turns a font into paths with OpenType.js and animates them with Framer Motion. A short handwritten sign-off supplied by Soren would make the treatment personal. Use a traced SVG stroke and a one-time CSS draw near the bio or Contact, with the completed mark visible in the static state. [Componentry](https://componentry.dev/docs/components/signature) |
| **ShaderGradient** | **First background study.** Save a preset with a charcoal palette, quiet depth, and fine grain. Render a still for a hero or section header and evaluate how it reads behind text. If live movement adds enough, the upstream React renderer accepts preset URLs or props and exposes grain and pixel density controls. [ShaderGradient](https://shadergradient.co/) · [Customizer](https://www.shadergradient.co/customize) · [Renderer documentation](https://github.com/ruucm/shadergradient#usage) |
| **WebGL Liquid** | **Alternate background study.** Its raw WebGL shader already has grain and palette controls. Try a few discrete luminance levels and add restrained dither around those steps for a more digital surface. Compare it with the corresponding ShaderGradient still on the same composition. [Componentry](https://componentry.dev/docs/components/webgl-liquid) |
| **Dithered Logo** | **Optional Ø treatment.** The canvas converts an image into dots and displaces them near the pointer. Test the effect on a large Ø moment or use a pre-rendered dithered mark as a still. Keep the shared Home control predictable and recognizable. [Componentry](https://componentry.dev/docs/components/dithered-logo) |
| **Cursor-driven Particle Typography** | **Optional decorative study.** The spring-back is worth borrowing for a standalone glyph or display word. Use a sparse particle count and a resolved still on touch and reduced motion. The main heading stays in HTML. [Componentry](https://componentry.dev/docs/components/cursor-driven-particle-typography) |
| **Matrix Rain** | **Texture reference.** Borrow sparse glyph movement for a localized transition or small region of the ASCII field. Derive the glyphs from the Ø or the image and use short pulses to give the site its own rhythm. [Componentry](https://componentry.dev/docs/components/matrix-rain) |

## Hero reel states

1. **Arrival.** Fill the hero with a responsive still derived from the matching reel frame. Use the ShaderGradient study as a reference for charcoal grain and depth. Keep the image recognizable through stepped luminance and restrained dithering, with ASCII concentrated in selected areas. Keep Soren’s name, intro, **View work**, and **Play reel** on a steady, readable layer.
2. **Reveal.** Align the still and reel so recognizable features stay in place through the handoff. Try an ordered reveal in tile groups or short horizontal bands, with a brief compression-like displacement at the boundary. Resolve once into clean footage and keep the title and controls free of distortion. Compare a short arrival beat before muted playback with a play-on-demand version. A fine-pointer preview can be evaluated separately after the basic handoff works.
3. **Playback and fallback.** Make **Play reel** start playback directly on touch and keyboard, and keep pause available. Hold the composed still while video loads or when autoplay is blocked. Keep a retry control after playback fails. For reduced motion, show the still and complete the handoff without a transition when the visitor explicitly starts playback. [Media playback guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay)

Compare with the existing full-width reel, full-width gradient, and combined ASCII study using the same copy and footage. Check whether the treated still feels like the opening frame of the reel and whether the phone crop preserves a recognizable focal point.

**Approved reveal study.** Open on the ski-goggle reflection in stepped charcoal with fine grain and a sparse glyph field. Retain the Ø from the desktop frame and center the reflection on phones. Give the still about a second to register before muted playback. When video is ready, reveal motion in the reflection first, then let neighboring bands briefly slip and resolve into color over roughly half a second. Keep **Play reel** available from arrival to start sooner. Evaluate the media transition on its own before revisiting typing or pointer effects.

## Work and Media

Keep the filterable Work grid as the project index, with visible titles, shareable case-study links, and a reliable return to the selected category, scroll position, and card focus. A featured carousel can showcase a small subset on Home or above the library if it adds value in review. Use deliberate navigation, meaningful slide labels, and visible keyboard-operable controls. [W3C carousel guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/carousels/)

Start Media as a proposed `/media/` route for photography, video, and art. Build a finite, image-led HTML gallery with responsive thumbnails, meaningful alt text, and project or media links where appropriate. Give video items a poster and playback controls. If the fisheye wall is worth keeping, make it an optional Explore view from the same entries. Map repeated canvas tiles back to the same item so each piece has one identity and remains easy to find in the regular gallery. Review how Media fits the navigation once representative assets are in place. [Route map](PORTFOLIO_ROUTE_MAP.md)

## Integration steps

1. **Shared foundation.** Keep the site’s static Astro build. Use Astro markup, CSS, and small TypeScript modules for clipped text, SVG strokes, scroll snap, and a low-resolution canvas. Share the existing motion and focus tokens. Let a project or explicitly selected media item supply `--color-accent` when a readable color is available. Keep the current shared fallback for monochrome content. [Astro client scripts](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/client-side-scripts/) · [Shared style guide](redesign/SHELL.md)
2. **Home and About.** Use the standalone ASCII, kinetic text, and full-width studies to build and review a screen-wide still-to-reel handoff. Review the media reveal before layering in title motion or pointer effects. Treat the signature as a small About detail and use the ShaderGradient still to inform the hero texture.
3. **Work and Media.** Complete the Work return flow first. Build a small Media sample with a photo, a video, and an artwork before testing the fisheye wall. Review the carousel as a separate featured study against the existing Work grid.
4. **Advanced effects.** Inspect the individual registry source before adapting it. Componentry supplies React source through shadcn, and its source repository lists an MIT license. Preserve applicable notices and verify the terms for upstream code, fonts, and demo assets. The carousel registry entry uses Three.js and GSAP. ShaderGradient v2’s React renderer uses React Three Fiber and Three.js with additional peers. If one of these studies earns the runtime cost, mount it as a local Astro React island and keep the static fallback outside it. Lazy-load optional viewers on activation or visibility as appropriate. [Componentry registry](https://componentry.dev/docs/mcp) · [Componentry license](https://github.com/harshjdhv/componentry/blob/main/LICENSE) · [GSAP terms](https://gsap.com/licensing/) · [ShaderGradient dependencies](https://github.com/ruucm/shadergradient#installation) · [Astro React integration](https://v6.docs.astro.build/en/guides/integrations-guide/react/)
5. **Review the composed pages.** Test desktop and a real phone with touch, pointer, keyboard, and reduced motion. Compare the opening with the current build on slow media and with WebGL unavailable. Keep labels and navigation usable while assets load, pause decorative render loops offscreen or when the tab is hidden, and check that stacked effects leave the site responsive. Recheck **Home → Work → PØRTAL → Back** after adding any motion.

## Earlier visual references

- **Apple and Rauno Freiberg.** Take consistent material behavior and purposeful, interruptible motion as references for how the interface should respond. Apply those principles to the portfolio’s own panel shapes, typography, and visual rhythm. [Apple Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials) · [Invisible Details of Interaction Design](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)
- **Ordinary Folk, Outpace Studios, and BLOND.** Use an obvious path from the opening to the archive, concise descriptions that connect design and technology, and an image-led index with a steady rhythm. [Ordinary Folk](https://www.ordinaryfolk.co/work) · [Outpace Studios](https://outpacestudios.com/) · [BLOND](https://www.blond.cc/)
- **Sweater Weather, Planestate, Lab, and Gravity Harps.** Use whitespace and strong grayscale surfaces to frame one subject, align project facts with tactile imagery, and give one accent a clear job. Interleave process images with explanations where a case study calls for depth. [Screenshot notes in the brief](PORTFOLIO_REDESIGN_BRIEF.md#visual-identity-and-references) · [Gravity Harps](https://andycavatorta.com/gravityharps.html)

## Status after the identity study

The retro-futurist terminal language is settled as **Artifact with texture** (see the [style guide](redesign/SHELL.md#chosen-direction-artifact-with-texture)). The ASCII, kinetic text, ShaderGradient and Matrix Rain studies now serve that direction as texture and hero treatments rather than as separate looks. The Liquid Glass Carousel is the Media Featured strip. Ordinary Folk is the model for the Work index.
